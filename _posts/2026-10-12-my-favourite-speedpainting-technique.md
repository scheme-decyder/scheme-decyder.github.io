Everyone seems to have their own speedpainting method. This is the method that I've found works for me best. It combines both speed while still looking decent, and layering techniques so it hides each individual method. As with a lot of my 'speedpainting' methods, it still takes a while for each model.

To find out how fast it was, I decided to time each step. This also lets you see what step had the biggest impact compared to the amount of time it takes.

For this, I am painting a Farrow force to be used for demo games for Warmachine. These models are STL's that are a part of the welcome pack of the [Warmachine MyMiniFactory|https://www.myminifactory.com/users/Warmachine].

A overview of the method is:
* Airbrush undertones
* Speedpaints
* Enamel Wash
* Quick Hightlights
* Effects

The total time is summarised in the table below. These models were done as a batch, the time taken is to complete that step on all models.

The main departure from my standard speedpaint method was the undershading process. Normally I will do something like rattlecan black, airbrush a dark colour like purple all over, then zenithal airbrush white ink over the top. I prefer to rattlecan my undercoat as I find the durability to be better than airbrush priming. However I don't like buying coloured rattlecans as they're so expensive for once off projects like this. White ink is also very fragile, so I tend to have to varnish it. This ends up being a lot of steps and a large amount of time for what is meant to be a quick process. I decided to shorten the steps by rattlecan-ing the lightest colour first, in this case white, then doing a reverse zenithal of purple and pink. I also applied pink to areas like the knuckles and ears. When the contrast paints go over this area you get the pink and purple undertones coming through. You don't get the "local" shadows in the small crevices, however the future enamel wash will shade these areas. I found this undershading method much more enjoyable and would do it again. But maybe that's just because I hate airbrushing white.

Another pro of white rattlecan is that you can use speedpaints directly on the base.

The most time consuming step was to apply the speedpaints. I tend to be fairly deliberate so there isn't much cleanup, but this means it takes a while. My biggest regret is the colour of the cloth, although I like the colour, it ended up being too close to the flesh tone with the pink undershading.

During painting disaster struck! A paint bottle dropped on Midas and his tusk and eyebrow flew off. Luckily I managed to find it on the floor. A bit of glue and you would never notice.

Looking at the timing table, there isn't a whole lot I would cut. It's unfortunate that I had to spend 30 minutes touching up the white on the bone areas from overspray, but that is a consquence of the underpaining method. An hour could be cut without the manual off-white highlights, but I like putting in that little bit of effort. I also find that adding the brushstrokes helps to hide the 'speedpaint' look. The enamel wash helps with this too.

As always, the biggest impact for least time is the finishing touches on the bases. Adding grass tufts and pigment powders is always a winner! Basing remains one of my favourite parts of painting a model.

The total time taken for these was 12 hours. This includes all the basing and varnishing. Definitely not speedpainting, but doable in a week or two. They look nice on the table and work well enough for a demo force.
